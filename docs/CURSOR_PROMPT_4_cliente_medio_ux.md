Who it is for and the four rules
----
The readers are the store director and the department managers, on a desktop in the back office and on a phone at 8 in the morning. Not analysts.
1. One question per screen. Three screens: **Oggi** (how was yesterday, in 4 numbers and 3 things to look at), **Reparti** (where the average customer spends time, the floorplan is the navigation), **Casse e banchi** (where people wait). Everything else — behaviour modes, adjacency matrix, flow arrows, data quality, reconciliation KPIs — goes under a fourth tab **Dettagli tecnici**, visible only to superadmin / Hyperspace personas, using the existing card components.
2. Numbers read like a person would say them: durations as `17 min 36 s`, `38 s`, `1 min 1 s` (helper `fmtDuration(minutes)`), counts with Italian thousands separator, no decimals below the minute except people-in-waiting (`0,7`). Every number has a plain sentence next to it (the "so what"), never a bare label. No unit-less percentages without context.
3. Verdicts, not data: each screen opens with the number and a coloured one-line reading (`warn` orange when a threshold is crossed: queue > 1 min per customer in a slot, counter ≥ 1 person waiting on average in a slot; neutral otherwise). Thresholds in config (`clienteMedio.thresholds`).
4. Empty means empty: counters without coverage (`zero_observation_rois`) render as dashed placeholder cards ("Senza sensori sopra il banco. Numeri disponibili dopo il sopralluogo."), never as 0. A KPI with `status='unreliable'` renders "—" with the reason in the tooltip. Comparisons with previous days render "Confronto disponibile da <weekday>" until ≥ 7 days exist in `daily_kpi`; never invent a trend.
